# desktop

Rust: the GPUI app for Windows and macOS. It draws screens and reaches the system (tray, notifications, starting at login, screenshots, the clipboard, the updater), and leaves everything else to the [core](../core/), which it links.

GPUI comes from GPUI Kit (`gpui-kit`), pinned exactly in `Cargo.toml`. See [PLAN.md: Desktop on GPUI](../PLAN.md#desktop-on-gpui-what-to-know).

## Building

You need a recent stable Rust (`rustup update stable`) and `calx` on PATH. `build.rs` builds the core (`core/lib.calx`) into a static library with `calx build --staticlib` and links it, so `cargo` is the only command:

```sh
cargo run --release
```

Set `CALX` to use a different `calx` binary.

## What's here

- `src/main.rs`: starts GPUI, loads Geist (in `assets/fonts`, under the OFL), puts GPUI Kit's widgets in loom's colours, and opens the window with loom's own title bar.
- `src/core.rs`: the core's C boundary. Its events come on the core's thread and go to the window over a channel.
- `src/model.rs`: what the screens show, as the core tells it. Lists come from the core's cache with `core.get` and are fetched again when an event says they changed. Only a reply still being written lives here alone, from its deltas.
- `src/app.rs`: the window. It has the sidebar of agents, under the knot and the wordmark, where an agent whose reply came while its chat wasn't open is bold until it is, the header with the agent's menu and its permission mode, and the chat. The chat is never swapped out for another page: an agent's own pages open in a panel beside it on the right, where its browser shows too, under its thread, which fidgets while the agent is idle: a thinking loop, a knot or a twang every few seconds (its edit page, under the thread with its colour beneath it, twelve of loom's, then a small colour wheel for its own, which opens a bigger wheel in a popup to pick from, with a field for one as #rrggbb, then its name and its instructions; its model, effort, permission, what a message while it answers does and its backup, each a row of chips with the person's default marked as one of them, which it follows while that one is on; what it knows, what it did (its activity) and its routines; every revision to go back to; and where it runs: Isolated, in a sandbox, which a member's agents always are and an instance without sandboxes can't be, and its network from there, Everything, Only these hosts (an editor that adds and removes them) or Nothing; its activity page: what it ran, what it was allowed, and what changed; and its memory page: its notes and the shared ones, to add to or delete), and everything else opens in a popup over the window, its pages down its left. At the foot of the sidebar is the server, as a soft ball of yarn: pale in its colour, with a few strands of it wound round, both chosen from its name, so each server looks like itself (`thread::yarn`). It rests while it's connected, its strands turn while it connects, it greys while it can't be reached, and signed out it's a dashed ring, with its name and how it is beside it. The app signs in to several servers, each with a core of its own and its own folder under `servers/` (kept in `server-list`): each is its yarn in that row, the one on screen with its name, another coming on screen when clicked, with a dot when one of its agents said something. The server popup's first page says the server's version, and quietly, a newer loom that's out (a small ring on its yarn in the sidebar too, from the core's `core.server`) or an update under way, with **Update** (or **Now** while one waits, and **Try again** after one failed) for an owner or admin, and to the owner, **Updates itself**, a switch (`instance.updates`). It also has **Scripts**: turned on, it shows the script token once, to copy, with the `loom ask` line to try, and it makes a new one or turns them off (`scripts.get`, `scripts.on`, `scripts.off`). Its **Add a server** signs in beside the one on screen, and **Sign out and remove** takes one away. When an agent's `add_server` is allowed here (the core's `core.offer`), Add a server opens with its address in, and with a link code, already signing in with it through a core for that server (`link`), so the yes in the chat was all: the server comes on screen once it's in, and a code that didn't work leaves the form saying why. While anything loom runs is behind, this app or any server, a quiet update icon sits just left of the gear: a click updates everything the person may update, servers first and the app last, and hovering it shows a small popup of each device that's behind with its own Update, or plainly why it can't be updated where it runs. The first core decides all of it (`core.updates`), and the app hands frames between its cores as that core asks (`route_updates`). **Settings**, at the other end of the row from the server, opens the popup at the person's pages, under the headings You, Agents and Work, with a search over them and what's on them (`settings.rs`), and the server at the server's. **Appearance**, **Chat** and **App & notifications** are this device's own, kept in `prefs.json` (`prefs.rs`): the theme, the chat's text size and the fonts (Geist or the system's, for words and for code), how wide the chat is (Normal, Comfortable or Full width; an empty chat's composer stays at Normal), whether an agent's replies show as bubbles in a breath of its colour, its corner by the thread squared off (the default), or as plain text, how much of an agent's tool calls a reply shows (hidden by default, folded, or every call), thoughts hidden (the default), folded or open, Ctrl+Enter to send, whether the sidebar shows search (hidden by default; Ctrl+F searches either way), starting at login, staying in the tray, keeping the computer awake while an agent works (`SetThreadExecutionState`), notifications, their previews and sound, and updates, checked from there or put in on their own while loom is in the tray and idle. **About you** is the person's profile, which every agent starts with, to edit. **Rules** (`rules.rs`) lists the person's rules on what agents touch, for every agent and then each agent's own, each in the instance's words with what it does and its note, to remove, and **New rule** makes one: allow, ask or deny; on a command, a path, a site, a tool or anything; what the action does; who started the turn; and for every agent or one. Its **What if** asks the instance what an action would get, and which rule or mode decided (`rule.check`), and the list follows `rules.updated`. **Routines** lists the routines, with when each runs next and how its last run went, to make, change, pause, run now or delete, and with a webhook, its address and secret to copy. A message a routine sent shows "From the routine …" in the chat. **Skills** lists the person's skills, to add, change or delete. **Proposals** lists the skills and notes agents propose, and the skills they write from a task the person recorded, each to keep or drop there, or to open, read with what the person did beside it, change and keep (`proposal.*`); **Ask** asks the agent on screen to look over its chats for more. **Tables** lists the tables their agents share, with who made each and how many rows, and opens one to its newest 200 rows, each with who wrote it and, when it was another agent than the owner, that it's from another agent; a table is dropped after a second click (`table.list`, `table.rows`, `table.drop`, and `table.changed` to reload). And **Agent programs** lists the instance's ACP agents, with their settings, and their settings shared with everyone else, a number of tokens each a day or a month (or for a member, what's left of theirs), and the ACP Registry's, to install, update or remove. **MCP servers** sets up the person's MCP servers and the keys they use, which each agent picks on its edit page. **Plugins** lists the person's plugins, with what each asks for and Allow for what waits, and opens a plugin's panel beside the chat (`src/panel.rs`), which shrinks to nothing while the popup is open, since a web view draws over everything in the window. It answers the panel's door: `data.get` and `data.put` with the plugin's data, and `cmd` for a command the plugin asked for and the person allowed. **This PC** (when the instance is somewhere else) sets what agents may do on this computer: run commands, read files, list folders, open things, take screenshots and use the clipboard, each off, ask or allow. It does the screenshots (with `xcap`) and the clipboard when the core asks (`core.device_ask`). **Hosts**, beside it, lists the person's own servers this computer reaches with ssh, each with Off, Ask or Allow and Remove, a field to add one by what ssh is given (`vps`, or `root@vps`), and under Found, the ones the core found in `~/.ssh/config` and on the tailnet, each with Add (`hosts.scan`, `host.add`, `host.set`, `host.remove`). The core keeps them and runs their commands. **People** (for an instance somewhere else) lists everyone with an account, to make one an admin, make them a password reset link, or remove them, and the invites still open, and makes an invite: a member or an admin, with its link to copy. **Add a phone** draws the QR code a phone scans to join the account (with `qrcode`), says when it has, and lists the devices signed in, to sign one out. On an instance somewhere else it can also delete the account, after asking (`account.delete`). For the instance in the app, it first asks to let phones reach this computer (`instance.phones`), and can stop them again. What went wrong, and good news such as a copied prompt, shows for a few seconds at the window's foot, over a popup too, so a click in Settings says what it did where it was clicked. The chat shows replies, each as one answer at the end: while the turn runs, one quiet line says the step it's on, and once it's done, its last words are the reply, with what it said and did on the way folded under one line that opens (`model::parts`), and a turn stopped or failed keeps everything in view. Bubbles from the same sender one after another join, their inner corners smaller (`model::corners`). It shows thoughts, the agent's plan as a list of its steps, tool calls (a turn that only used tools the person hides says it used them, in a quiet line), questions, files and pictures, and the composer, which takes files from its plus menu (which rises into place just above the composer, with skills and routines beside), dropped on the chat, or pasted (an image, or files copied in Explorer). Search in the sidebar finds words in every chat, and in file names. Every launch opens with one thread drawing itself across the middle, the mark settling on it, and the window opening outwards along that line to show the app, in under two seconds (`splash`). The welcome, with the threads woven through the name, starts the instance here or signs in to a server, and making the first agent fills the window too: pick a brain (an agent program, as a tile whose thread moves under the pointer), a name and a colour, and the threads weave it together, then the window closes on its thread into its chat. Another agent, from the sidebar's +, is made the same way beside the sidebar. A page beside the sidebar unrolls under a thread passing down it, in the agent's colour for a chat. An agent's empty chat twangs its thread hello, thinks along while the person types to it, and brings its suggestions in one by one. While an agent's browser is open, the page shows as it changes (`browser.watch`, `browser.frame`) in a small window at the chat's bottom right, which a click, or the **Browser** button in its header, opens in the panel beside the chat, and whose X hides it until the browser next opens. Opening anything else in the panel puts the browser back in its small window. The panel's left edge drags to make it wider or narrower, and the app keeps its width (`side-width`). The sidebar collapses to a rail, from the control beside its + or Ctrl+B, and the app keeps that too (`sidebar`, `rail.rs`): each agent as its thread, with a dot while a reply waits and its name under the pointer, the +, and at the foot the update icon over the gear over the server's yarn, each opening what it opens in the whole sidebar. A window too narrow for the sidebar and the chat makes it a rail on its own, and it comes back once there's room, unless the person collapsed it. Its width eases between the two. and **Take over** gives the person the page: their clicks, scrolls and keys go to it (`browser.input`) until **Hand back**, with a **Record** switch beside it that records what they do there to teach the agent the task (`browser.record`), saying how many steps it has. When the agent asks for them (`browser_ask_person`), a card above the composer says what for and opens it, and when it asks for something only they should know (`browser_ask_secret`), a masked box there sends it straight into the page (`browser.secret`). **Ask on the side**, in the agent's menu, asks it about the chat beside it (`chat.ask`), with the question and the answer shown there and neither in the chat. **Runs on**, under This chat at the foot of the agent's edit page, shows the brains, the one the chat runs on filled, and moves the chat to another (`conversation.move`), and **Start fresh** under it asks for a second click. The agent's menu itself is short: pin, edit, ask on the side and delete. Its next reply starts with a quiet line that says so, as one does when the agent's backup took a turn over (a `notice` block). The agent's edit page picks its **Backup program** (the default's, none, or another brain), and the Defaults page the backup every agent without one follows. A message is pinned from the pin in its corner (`message.pin`), and the header's pin, with how many there are, opens the chat's pins beside it (`messages.pinned`), each to jump to or unpin. The branch in a message's corner branches the chat there (`conversation.branch`): the branch opens in the chat's place, under a strip with its title and a way back, and the chat lists its branches under the messages they go on from. A page an agent showed with `html_render` (a `text/html` file) is a card in the chat that opens the page beside it, in the same walled-off web view as a plugin's panel, with nothing through its door. A card an agent put in its reply with `show_card` is drawn in the chat as part of it (`card_view`): choices as a row of buttons in the agent's colour, which send the one clicked as the person's message while that reply is the newest, and a checklist, a table, progress or facts in a quiet box, each changing in place as `card` frames come. A message sent while the agent is still answering waits above the composer (`queue.changed`), where each can go now (steer), move up, be edited or be dropped. **Tasks** in Settings starts a long job for an agent, in a conversation of its own, and lists every task with what it's doing, to pause, resume, cancel or try again. Opening one shows its conversation in the chat, under a strip with its title, its status and a way back to the agent's chat (`task.updated`). Esc steps back: out of a menu, the popup, the panel beside the chat, the server form, or a step of making an agent. Threads ease between moods and colours (`thread.rs` keeps each one's last under a key), switches slide, hovers fade in and out rather than snapping and a pressed control steps a shade further (`motion.rs`), words on an agent's colour take whichever ink reads on it, small things fade in, and screens change through the threads, never by fading. A chat with nothing in it yet puts the composer in the middle, under the agent's thread and name, with a few things to ask. Menus are an icon and a title, with no explanations under them. The look follows `design/explorations/round-4.html`, with every value from `design/tokens.toml` (`design/generated/tokens.rs`).
- `src/rules.rs`: the Rules page in the popup: the rules, the new-rule form and the what-if.
- `src/thread.rs`: an agent's thread, drawn as one path that moves with what the agent is doing.
- `src/shell.rs`: the tray (Open, Start at login, Quit), Windows notifications for what the core says is worth one, starting at login with `--background`, hiding the window to the tray when it's closed, and putting the window back where it was (`put_window`, `window_place`). A tick that comes long after the last means the computer slept, so the app tells the core (`app.wake`). It also turns a pasted bitmap into a PNG.
- `src/place.rs`: where the window was. loom opens where it was closed, the same size, maximized if it was, kept in `window.json` each time the window moves or resizes, so a crash or an update keeps it too. A place whose title bar is on no screen now, as when a monitor was unplugged, comes back centered on the screen it overlapped most, or the main one, made to fit.
- `src/panel.rs`: panels (PLAN.md, "The panel sandbox"), each in a WebView2 web view of its own over the window, through `wry`. A panel loads from its own scheme and origin (`panel-<id>`), and its pages come with a content security policy that lets them load only their own files. WebView2 also answers every other request with 403 before it reaches the network, and the browser sends whatever's left to a proxy that isn't there and looks up no host name. Navigation away, new windows, permissions, downloads and drops are refused. A prelude in every frame takes away the web view's storage and WebRTC, which nothing else stops in WebView2, and gives the panel `loom.send(op, args)`: the one door, whose messages the app checks.
- `src/panel_test.rs`: `loom --panel-test OUT`, the break-out test CI runs. It shows `panels/breakout`, a panel that tries 30 ways out (fetch, XHR, WebSocket, images, scripts, frames, workers, forms, windows, WebRTC in its own window and a blank frame's, storage, the core through the door, navigating away), while listening on a TCP and a UDP port of this machine that the panel aims at. It passes when every attempt is stopped and nothing reaches the listeners.
- [`setup/`](setup/): `loom-setup.exe`, loom's installer, updater and uninstaller, a program of its own, drawn by hand in loom's look rather than with GPUI so it stays small and opens at once.


## Trying it

Run an instance (`calx server/main.calx serve`), then start the app and sign in with its address, a username, a password and, the first time, the setup code it printed. Or choose **Run one on this computer** on the sign-in page: the app keeps that choice in `mode` in its data folder, and runs the instance itself from then on, with its data in `instance` there. **Sign out** goes back to the sign-in page and stops it; its agents and chats stay for next time. While developing:

- `LOOM_SERVER`: an instance's address, filled in on the sign-in page.
- `LOOM_DATA`: the folder for the core's cache, instead of `%APPDATA%\Loom`.
- `LOOM_THEME`: `dark` or `light`, instead of following the system.
- `LOOM_UPDATE`: a release manifest's address, for trying updates in a build from source, which doesn't update itself otherwise.
- `LOOM_UPDATE_KEY`: the public key, in hex, a test's releases at `LOOM_UPDATE` are signed with. And `LOOM_TOKEN`: a device token to start with, instead of signing in. A build from source alone reads them.

## Measuring

`cargo build --release --features perf` builds an app that measures itself (`src/perf.rs`) and opens its window without taking the focus. Each second it draws anything, it adds a line to `perf.log` in its data folder: frames a second, the average and longest frame (from the start of `render` to the end of painting), and the time in each timed step: `render` (building the window's elements, the rest of a frame being GPUI's layout and painting), `chat`, `take` (a frame from the core), `rows` (rebuilding the chat's rows), and a count of each thing that asked for another frame (`want:app`, `want:motion`, `want:thread`, `want:yarn`). It also says when the core started, the first frame and the chat came. [`tests/bench/desktop.calx`](../tests/bench/desktop.calx) makes a data folder with a long chat to start it on, and streams a reply into it while it runs. Without the feature, none of this is built.

## Releases

A tag like `v0.3.0` runs `.github/workflows/release.yml`. It builds `loom.exe` with the version in it (`LOOM_VERSION`) and [`setup/`](setup/), loom's own installer and updater, then `scripts/release.calx pack` puts `loom.exe` into the setup as `loom-setup.exe`, under a header signed with the release key. It also builds the server for Linux, Windows and macOS, and publishes them all with a `manifest.json` that `scripts/release.calx` signs with the same key, from the CI secret `LOOM_RELEASE_KEY`. The tag's message is the release notes.

`loom-setup.exe` puts loom in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\loom`, for the person alone, with no administrator, and gives its Start menu shortcut the AppUserModelID `AaenStudios.Loom`, so notifications show as loom. From then on loom updates itself: the core finds a newer release, downloads its `loom-setup.exe` and checks it against the signed manifest, and the sidebar offers "Restart to update" (or it goes in on its own while loom is in the tray and no agent works, if the person turned that on). `shell::apply_update` hands the setup over with `--update` and the app quits. The setup checks its own signature again, swaps `loom.exe`, starts the new one and watches it start, and puts the old one back if it doesn't. "Check for updates" in the tray looks at once. See [setup/README.md](setup/README.md).
